PATIENT DISCHARGE INSTRUCTIONS — GENERAL GUIDELINES
Document Version: 1.4 | Effective Date: March 1, 2024

1. PURPOSE
These instructions provide general guidance for patients being discharged from
inpatient or outpatient care. Condition-specific discharge summaries will be
provided separately by your care team.

2. BEFORE YOU LEAVE THE FACILITY
- Ensure you have received and reviewed your written discharge summary.
- Collect all personal belongings, valuables stored by the facility, and any
  medical equipment issued to you.
- Confirm your follow-up appointment has been scheduled.
- Make sure all new prescriptions have been sent to your pharmacy or are in hand.
- Ask your nurse or discharge coordinator any outstanding questions before leaving.
- Arrange transportation — patients who received sedation or anesthesia must
  not drive for at least 24 hours.

3. MEDICATIONS AFTER DISCHARGE
- Take all prescribed medications exactly as instructed.
- Do not stop any medication without consulting your provider, even if you feel better.
- Keep a complete and updated list of all your medications, including over-the-counter
  drugs and supplements, and share it with every healthcare provider you visit.
- If you experience side effects, call the pharmacy helpline or your provider before
  stopping the medication.
- If any medications require refrigeration, make sure to store them properly upon
  arriving home.

4. WOUND AND INCISION CARE
- Keep the wound clean and dry for the first 24–48 hours unless instructed otherwise.
- Change dressings as directed by your nurse or surgeon.
- Watch for signs of infection: increased redness, warmth, swelling, pus, or fever
  above 38.5°C (101.3°F).
- Do not submerge the wound in water (baths, pools, hot tubs) until cleared by your
  provider.
- Avoid applying lotions, creams, or powders to the wound unless specifically prescribed.

5. ACTIVITY RESTRICTIONS
- Rest as needed, but light activity (short walks) is encouraged to prevent blood clots.
- Avoid strenuous exercise, heavy lifting (over 5 kg), or intense physical activity
  for the period specified in your condition-specific discharge summary.
- Do not drive if you are taking narcotic/opioid pain medications.
- Gradually resume normal activities as tolerated.

6. DIET AND NUTRITION
- Follow any specific dietary instructions provided by your dietitian or provider.
- Stay well-hydrated — aim for 6–8 glasses of water per day unless fluid-restricted.
- Avoid alcohol while taking antibiotics, opioids, sedatives, or blood thinners.
- If nausea is present, start with light foods: crackers, toast, broth, bananas.

7. FOLLOW-UP APPOINTMENTS
- Your first follow-up appointment is typically scheduled within 7–14 days of discharge.
- Do not skip follow-up appointments even if you feel completely recovered.
- Bring your discharge summary, medication list, and insurance card to the appointment.
- If you need to reschedule, call at least 24 hours in advance: 1-800-APPT-NOW.

8. WHEN TO SEEK EMERGENCY CARE
Go to the nearest emergency room or call 911 immediately if you experience:
- Chest pain or pressure
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Sudden numbness or weakness on one side of the body
- Severe uncontrolled bleeding
- High fever above 39.5°C (103°F) that does not respond to medication
- Confusion, disorientation, or loss of consciousness
- Severe abdominal pain or rigid abdomen
- Signs of allergic reaction: hives, throat swelling, difficulty swallowing

9. CONTACTING YOUR CARE TEAM
- For non-urgent questions: Use the patient portal messaging system (response within 24 hours).
- For urgent (non-emergency) concerns: Call the nurse advice line at 1-800-NURSE-1.
- For prescription questions: Call the pharmacy at 1-800-MED-HELP.
- After-hours urgent line: 1-800-AFTER-HR (available 24/7 for urgent clinical guidance).

10. PATIENT RIGHTS
- You have the right to request your medical records at any time.
- You may refuse any treatment and must be informed of the consequences.
- You have the right to a second opinion without affecting your care.
- All care decisions should be made collaboratively between you and your provider.
