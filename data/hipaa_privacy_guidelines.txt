HIPAA PRIVACY AND SECURITY GUIDELINES FOR PATIENTS
Document Version: 2.2 | Effective Date: January 1, 2024

1. WHAT IS HIPAA?
The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) is a federal law
that protects the privacy and security of your health information. It establishes
national standards for how healthcare providers, health plans, and their business
associates handle Protected Health Information (PHI).

2. WHAT IS PROTECTED HEALTH INFORMATION (PHI)?
PHI includes any individually identifiable health information, such as:
- Your name, address, date of birth, Social Security Number
- Medical record numbers, account numbers, insurance IDs
- Diagnosis, treatment history, lab results, prescriptions
- Billing and payment records related to healthcare
- Any information that can be used to identify you in the context of healthcare

3. YOUR RIGHTS UNDER HIPAA
As a patient, you have the following rights:
a) Right to Access: You can request a copy of your medical records. Requests
   are fulfilled within 30 days. A reasonable fee may apply for copies.
b) Right to Amend: You can request corrections to inaccurate or incomplete
   information in your medical record.
c) Right to Accounting of Disclosures: You can request a list of entities to whom
   your PHI was disclosed in the past 6 years.
d) Right to Restrict Disclosures: You may request restrictions on how your PHI is
   used or shared for treatment, payment, or operations.
e) Right to Confidential Communications: You may request that communications
   be sent to an alternate address or phone number.
f) Right to Complain: You may file a complaint with our Privacy Officer or with the
   U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) without fear of retaliation.

4. HOW WE USE AND SHARE YOUR INFORMATION
We may use and share your PHI in the following ways without your authorization:
- Treatment: Sharing with other providers involved in your care.
- Payment: Submitting claims to your insurance company.
- Healthcare Operations: Quality assessments, staff training, audits.
- Public Health Reporting: Reporting communicable diseases to public health authorities.
- Legal Requirements: Responding to court orders or government investigations.

We will NOT share your PHI for marketing purposes or sell your data without
your written authorization.

5. AUTHORIZATION REQUIREMENTS
Your written authorization is required before we may:
- Share PHI with employers or schools
- Use your PHI for marketing activities
- Share psychotherapy notes
- Use PHI for research not covered by a waiver
- Share PHI with individuals not listed in your authorized contacts

6. MINIMUM NECESSARY STANDARD
We are required to disclose only the minimum amount of PHI necessary to
accomplish the intended purpose of the use or disclosure.

7. BREACH NOTIFICATION
- In the event of a breach of unsecured PHI, affected patients will be notified
  within 60 days of discovery.
- Notification will include: what happened, types of information involved,
  steps patients should take, and what we are doing to investigate.
- Large breaches (500+ patients) are reported to HHS and may be reported to media.

8. ELECTRONIC HEALTH RECORDS (EHR) SECURITY
- All EHR systems are encrypted at rest and in transit using AES-256 and TLS 1.3.
- Access to EHR is role-based; staff can only access records relevant to their duties.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is required for all clinical staff.
- All access to patient records is logged and audited.
- Portable devices (laptops, tablets) used to access PHI must be encrypted.

10. PENALTIES FOR VIOLATIONS
HIPAA violations can result in civil penalties ranging from $100 to $50,000 per
violation, with an annual cap of $1.5 million. Criminal violations may result in
imprisonment up to 10 years.
